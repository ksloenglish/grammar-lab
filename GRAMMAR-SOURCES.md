# Grammar source notes

The explanations and original practice sentences are authored for this app. The following references informed formation and usage rules, accessed 27 October 2026.

Cambridge English Grammar Today explains short adjective -er/-est, -e/-r/-st, final-consonant doubling, consonant+y changes, longer more/most forms, irregulars, than and the. It allows some two-syllable alternatives and explains possessive determiners instead of the. Its example sentences are not reproduced in the question bank.
https://dictionary.cambridge.org/us/grammar/british-grammar/comparison-adjectives-bigger-biggest-more-interesting

Cambridge's farther/further guidance accepts both for distance; further is more common. Further, not farther, is used for additional information or discussing something more. Both superlative distance variants are valid.
https://dictionary.cambridge.org/us/grammar/british-grammar/farther-farthest-or-further-furthest

EF's adverb comparison reference describes short adverbs fast/hard/late, longer -ly more/most forms and well/badly/far irregulars. The app distinguishes early from regular -ly adverbs and accepts optional the in adverbial superlatives where appropriate.
https://www.ef.com/wwen/english-resources/english-grammar/comparative-and-superlative-adverbs/

All question contexts are illustrative school scenarios, not claims about actual Hong Kong places, people or current events.
